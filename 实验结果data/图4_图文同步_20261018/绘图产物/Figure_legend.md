Fig. 4. Web-source mixing risks and structural verification of source-separated output in CitrusQA.

(a) Judge-flagged hallucination rates in the legacy mixed-arbitration architecture. A denotes local-only control; B, C, C′ and D denote unfiltered mixing, receipt-level, model-visible and excerpt-only filtering. The same 48 questions were assigned to two LLM judges per condition. Rates use the frozen nominal denominator of 96 planned judge slots: 3.1% (3/96), 24.0% (23/96), 33.3% (32/96), 18.8% (18/96) and 22.9% (22/96). One D-arm Kimi judgment was empty (95 non-empty verdicts); the missing label was not imputed. Evidence-preview limitations constrain interpretation of the flagged rates.

(b) Structural verification in v11. Among 144 three-pool final responses, local sections contained no [Wn] markers or matches to the predefined URL/domain patterns; all 820 web-marker occurrences were in web sections. The left strip reports occurrence allocation; rows below report answer-level lexical checks. Targeted review of 51 distinct responses containing external-authority terms (263 sentence fragments) found no unsupported web-derived factual statements under the applied screening criteria. These were screened responses, not a random sample or exhaustive entailment audit. Pool C used web markers in 47/48 responses; the remaining response included web information only in its web section without [Wn]. Separately, all 1,649 local citation-marker occurrences in an 84-question system-level batch resolved to their answer-specific cited pools, with zero out-of-pool markers (right). Colored widths show numerators against their stated denominators; the empty baseline denotes zero. The panels represent different batches and do not estimate a causal reduction in hallucination rates. Structural and lexical compliance does not establish complete semantic isolation or claim-level entailment.
