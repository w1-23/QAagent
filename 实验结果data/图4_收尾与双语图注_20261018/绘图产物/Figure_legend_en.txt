Fig. 4. Web-source mixing risks and structural verification of source-separated output in CitrusQA.

(a) Judge-flagged hallucination rates in five evidence-handling conditions of the legacy mixed-arbitration architecture. A denotes local-only control; B, C, C′ and D denote unfiltered mixing, receipt-level, model-visible and excerpt-only filtering. The same 48 questions were assigned to two independent LLM judges per condition. Rates retain the frozen nominal denominator of 96 planned judge slots: 3.1% (3/96), 24.0% (23/96), 33.3% (32/96), 18.8% (18/96) and 22.9% (22/96). D had one empty judgment (95 non-empty verdicts); no missing label was imputed. Evidence-preview limitations constrain these pre-separation observations, which do not estimate a reduction in hallucination rates under the new architecture.

(b) Source-boundary and citation-integrity audits in v11. Among 144 three-pool final responses, local sections contained neither [Wn] markers nor matches to the predefined URL/domain patterns (0/144 each); web sections contained all 820 web-marker occurrences. Existing targeted review of 51 distinct keyword-screened responses (263 sentence fragments) found no unsupported web-derived factual statements under the applied criteria. All 48 time-sensitive-pool responses met the examined section-placement rules; 47 carried web markers, whereas C48-new-33 included web information only in its web section without a corresponding marker. In an independent 84-question system-level batch, all 1,649 local citation-marker occurrences resolved to their answer-specific cited pools, with zero out-of-pool markers. Answers, web-marker occurrences and local citation markers are reported separately, without a composite score. Structural and lexical compliance does not establish complete semantic isolation or claim-level evidential support.
